MARINE BIOMATERIALS

Possibilities beginning with marine-derived materials

New uses for resources begin with understanding the materials themselves.

Chitosan is a naturally derived polymer obtained from sources including crustaceans, and it has been studied in biomaterials, cosmetics and other fields. Its properties may vary with molecular characteristics, usage conditions and intended applications, making careful evaluation essential.

KAISUNCELL explores ways to connect the value of marine-derived resources with professional care. Rather than focusing solely on ingredients, we consider where products will be used and the experiences users need.

CORE TECHNOLOGY

Water-Soluble CTSF™

Connecting material properties with practical product opportunities.

Water-Soluble CTSF™ is a water-soluble biomaterial technology based on marine-derived chitosan. Its material characteristics provide a starting point for exploring different product formats and environments.

KAISUNCELL explores this technology for specialized skin, scalp and oral care products. Instead of disclosing the details of proprietary processes, we focus on potential applications and meaningful user experiences.

MATERIAL VALUE

Three perspectives on expanding material potential

New value emerges when technology meets real-world conditions.

Material Versatility

Potential applications across diverse care fields

Biomaterial applications are not limited to a single format or category. KAISUNCELL considers the characteristics of water-soluble chitosan-based materials and explores uses aligned with the different purposes of professional care.

Application-Oriented Development

Development informed by the conditions of use

The needs of a product vary according to the area of application and care routine. We consider the distinct environments of skin, scalp and oral care and connect material research with product planning for professional and everyday use.

Sustainable Perspective

Rethinking the value of marine resources

We explore new uses for marine resources, including crustacean by-products. By considering both material properties and product needs, we examine opportunities for resource circulation and communicate environmental outcomes cautiously, based on evidence.
